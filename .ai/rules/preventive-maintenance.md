# Preventive maintenance

## Plans schedule preventive work by time only
REQ-17 adds the tenant tables `preventive_maintenance_plans`, `preventive_maintenance_executions` and `preventive_execution_status_transitions` (migrations `2026_10_25_000001` to `000004` in `database/migrations/tenant`). A plan (asset_card_id, maintenance_category_id, title, description, instructions, frequency_type, interval, starts_at, ends_at, responsible_id, created_by_id, status) repeats by calendar time only: `PreventiveFrequencyType` is Once, Daily, Weekly, Monthly or Yearly plus an `interval` >= 1, so it fits any asset, not just usage-metered machinery. The category must be active and the responsible must have the technician role. `ends_at` is optional; a Once plan always stores interval 1 and ends_at = starts_at. `PreventivePlanStatus` is Active, Paused, Finished or Cancelled and only changes through `transitionTo()`; Finished is set only by the recurrence service (`markAsFinished()`), never by hand.

## Executions are the dated work and keep their own audit trail
`PreventiveMaintenanceExecution` is one concrete action, derived from a plan (preventive_maintenance_plan_id) or created as a one-off with a null plan. It copies asset, category, title, description and instructions from the plan and is assigned to a technician (assigned_to_id). `status` (Scheduled, InProgress, Completed, Skipped, Cancelled) is not fillable and changes only through `transitionTo()`, which writes a row in `preventive_execution_status_transitions` inside a transaction; the `created` hook writes the initial row. Skipped and Cancelled require a reason, Completed requires `execution_notes`, and `transitionTo()` sets started_at, completed_at and completed_by_id. "Overdue" is computed (`isOverdue()`, `scopeOverdue`), never stored. A unique index on (plan_id, scheduled_at) prevents two executions of a plan on the same date; one-off executions have a null plan and may repeat dates.

## Postponing keeps the original date so the plan never regenerates it
`postpone()` is an action, not a status: it only works on Scheduled executions, needs a reason, a date after the current one that is not in the past, and a date no other execution of the same plan holds. It moves `scheduled_at`, leaves `original_scheduled_at` untouched and records the change in the audit trail with the same status on both sides. `reassignTo()` works on Scheduled and InProgress executions and records the new technician the same way. `original_scheduled_at` is filled from `scheduled_at` when the execution is created and is what the recurrence service uses to know which dates of a plan already exist.

## RecurrenceGenerator creates executions explicitly and idempotently
`App\Services\PreventiveMaintenance\RecurrenceGenerator::occurrences()` is a pure function (frequency, interval, starts_at, ends_at, from, until) that computes every date from `starts_at` and never from the previous one, so month-end clamping does not accumulate (31 Jan, 28 Feb, 31 Mar; yearly 29 Feb falls on 28 Feb in non-leap years). `generate($plan, $today = null)` only runs for Active plans, creates executions from today (never in the past) up to today + `HORIZON_DAYS` (90, both ends included, so 91 dates), skips every date already present in `original_scheduled_at` (postponed, skipped, cancelled or completed ones included), and creates each one through `PreventiveMaintenanceExecution::create()` so the audit hook fires, catching `UniqueConstraintViolationException` for concurrent requests. A plan whose `ends_at` already passed is finished instead; a plan without `ends_at` is never finished by the service. Pausing stops generation only. Generation runs on plan creation, on reactivation and on `POST /preventive-plans/{plan}/occurrences`, never on a GET, and it must not be called inside `DB::transaction()` because a caught unique violation aborts a PostgreSQL transaction.

## Preventive routes and who can use them
Plans: `GET /preventive-plans` (optional ?status=), `/create`, `POST`, `GET /{plan}`, `/{plan}/edit`, `PUT /{plan}`, `PATCH /{plan}/status` (Active, Paused or Cancelled only) and `POST /{plan}/occurrences`. Executions: `GET /preventive-executions` (optional ?status= and ?assigned=me), `/create`, `POST` (one-off), `GET /{execution}`, `PATCH /{execution}/status`, `PATCH /{execution}/schedule` (postpone) and `PATCH /{execution}/assignment`. tenant_admin and maintenance_chief manage everything; a technician sees only the executions assigned to them (and the plans where they are responsible or have an execution), and can only do Scheduled→InProgress and →Completed on their own execution; operators see everything read-only. Controllers share `Concerns/ResolvesPreventiveAccess`. Authorization runs before validation, `allowed_transitions`, `can_postpone`, `can_reassign`, `can_manage` and `can_generate` are computed in the server, and domain errors redirect back with the 'error' flash while successes use 'success'.

## A plan's schedule is fixed once it exists
`PreventivePlanController::update()` only accepts title, description, instructions, maintenance_category_id, responsible_id and ends_at (not for Once plans); frequency_type, interval and starts_at never change after creation. To change the calendar, cancel the plan and create a new one. A new responsible applies to the executions generated from then on, not to the ones already scheduled. Finished and Cancelled plans cannot be edited, and cancelling a plan stops generation but does not cancel its existing executions. The Inertia pages (`PreventiveMaintenance/Plans/*` and `PreventiveMaintenance/Executions/*`) are rendered by these controllers and implemented by the frontend PR.
